GeoAI: how phone photos and scooter video become checked, mapped road-condition records

GeoAI is a capstone project from B.M.S. College of Engineering, Bengaluru. Citizens photograph damaged roads, and a phone on a scooter records video of whole streets. A vision-language model, an AI that reads an image and answers in words, decides whether each image shows damage, which kinds and how bad. Results it is unsure about go to a human expert. Everything ends up on a map, and for any stretch of road the system can write a condition report with a road-condition score under the Indian Roads Congress code IRC:82-2023.

This page explains every part in plain English: what it does, why it exists, what the real numbers are, and where it is weak. Every number comes from a file in the repository; the sources are listed in a comment at the end of the page.

1The problem in one picture

Bengaluru has thousands of kilometres of road and too few engineers to walk them. Today a road is inspected by eye, the notes stay on paper, and a complaint from a citizen arrives as a sentence with no photo or location. Repairs wait because nobody knows exactly where the damage is or how bad it is.

GeoAI makes every observation a record with three parts that stay together: the image, the model's answer with how sure it is, and the place on the map.

AI model fixed code and rules storage people and their devices warning or weakness

The only outputs the system can give

For one photo or one 5 m video segment, the answer is always one of these:

OutputWhen
Rejected: not a road (rejected_non_pavement)Citizen photo only. The pre-check is sure the image is not pavement.
NormalThe model sees no damage, and is at least 80% sure.
Distress with types, severity and a descriptionTypes come from 18 named IRC:82 damage types; severity is Low, Medium or High (or none where the code gives none).
Sent to expert reviewAny answer the model is less than 80% sure of, an unreadable answer, or a confident "Normal" that a second check disagrees with.
Skipped or not coveredVideo only. A skipped segment was judged uninformative by a cheap filter; a not-covered segment had no usable frame. Neither is ever counted as Normal.

On top of these, a condition report for a chosen stretch gives chainage (distance along the road) and a road-condition score from 0 to 100 with a 90% interval (section 7).

How a citizen photo arrives

The app uploads the photo to Cloudinary and a row to Supabase. A database trigger creates an assessment row marked pending. The worker claims pending rows in arrival order, five at a time, using a database lock (FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED) so two workers never take the same job. If a download or the database fails, it retries up to 3 times with a growing wait, then marks the row failed. Uploads are capped at 10 MB and 4,096 pixels a side.

How a drive arrives

The capture app records compressed video, a timestamp for every frame, the satellite position about once a second (GNSS, the general name for GPS-type systems) and the phone's motion sensors 200 times a second (IMU: accelerometer and gyroscope). It also writes a manifest with a checksum of every file, so a damaged upload is caught before processing. The phone was mounted on a scooter, not a car; this matters for the roughness estimate later.

Why it matters

Without one shared clock, a frame could be placed tens of metres away from where it was taken. The pipeline uses the sensor clock of each frame, not the frame number, because phones drop and repeat frames.

5The photo pipeline, step by step

Each citizen photo passes through three questions to the model and one fixed rule. The video segments use the same model questions (from section 6 onward).

Rules in the order they fire

  1. Stage 0, pre-filter. The model is asked if the photo shows pavement. Only a clear "no" rejects it (17 of 361 uploads). Anything unclear goes on, because wrongly throwing away a road photo is worse than processing a non-road one.
  2. Stage 1, damaged or not. The model answers with one word. Its confidence c1 is the softmax of the two first-token scores, Normal against Distress. Softmax turns two raw scores into two probabilities that add up to 1. Stage 1 is allowed only 12 new tokens, because it needs one word; 200 tokens took about four times as long for the same answer.
  3. Stage 2, what kind. Only for Distress. The model returns a list of damage types, a severity and a short description. Its confidence is the geometric mean of the probabilities of the tokens in the type list only, not the description.
  4. Per-type probe (shadow). 20 yes/no questions, one per type, scored from the model's own yes/no probabilities in about 1.4 seconds. The scores are stored but do not change the labels yet.
  5. Safety net. If Stage 1 said Normal but the probe sees likely damage, the photo goes to review. The label is not changed.
  6. Gate. If Stage 1 and Stage 2 confidence are both at least 0.80, the result is classified; otherwise expert_review.
Key idea

The confidence that decides review uses only the tokens of the damage list. When it included the free-text description, confidently written prose scored as "sure" and the score ranked wrong answers above right ones (AUROC 0.373 against 0.744 for the field-only score on 407 labelled frames).

The field confidence, for the probabilities pi of the type-list tokens S, is

c_field = exp( (1/|S|) · Σi∈S log pi )

that is, the typical probability of a token in the list. A long list does not get a lower score just for being long.

Design vs build

Planned: once experts correct answers, inject corrections as examples into the prompt and retrain the model with LoRA periodically ("Phase 3"). Built: the expert page and the review log exist, but the prompt injection and retraining are not built, and the Retrain button is switched off (RETRAIN_ENABLED=false). The team later ruled out LoRA entirely.

6The video pipeline, step by step

Video gives far more coverage than citizen photos: a whole street, every 5 metres. The pipeline turns a drive into a list of segments, each with one frame and one answer.

Rules in the order they fire

  1. Validate. Check every file's checksum and the phone's orientation. A drive filmed in portrait is refused.
  2. Trajectory. Fit satellite time to real time, convert positions to local metres, and draw a smooth curve through them using position and speed.
  3. Segment. Walk along the curve and cut every 5 m of distance travelled. A last piece is kept only if it is at least 2.5 m.
  4. Pick a frame. Among the frames that show that 5 m, keep the sharpest, judged by blur from motion and rotation.
  5. Gate. A cheap filter scores the frame with DINOv2 (a general image-feature model), a small probe trained on Attain, and the length of crack-like lines. If the scores are low the segment is skipped, except a 10% random audit sample that is sent anyway.
  6. Model. Stage 1 and Stage 2 as for photos, with a "video profile" that skips record-only work.
  7. Store and verify. Write rows, compare counts and checksums, then delete temporary files.
Watch out

The gate barely filters. On the first three drives, where every segment was sent so it could be checked, the gate kept 92.1% of the model's damaged segments but also sent 90.9% of all segments. A stricter probe-only gate reached only 87.8% recall on held-out drives, below its 90% target, so it was not adopted.

Why it matters

A segment always has a state. If the gate skipped it, the map shows grey, not green. A road the system did not look at is never reported as healthy.

7The condition report: chainage and PCI

Engineers plan repairs by stretch and by kilometre, not by photo. In the video map a user clicks a start point A and an end point B. The server finds the path, measures distance along it, and scores the road.

Chainage is the distance along a road from a reference point, written like Km 0+260 (0 km and 260 m). GeoAI measures it from A, using the GPS track. The official road chainage, counted from the road's own start and marked on km-stones, is printed only if the user enters the km-stone value at A.

PCI (Pavement Condition Index) is a score from 0 (failed) to 100 (excellent). GeoAI follows IRC:82-2023, which computes it as a weighted sum of six per-parameter scores:

PCI = 0.40·roughness + 0.16·potholes + 0.14·rut depth + 0.12·cracking + 0.10·ravelling + 0.08·patching

Each term is that parameter's own 0-100 score, read off a curve printed in the code. The classes are Excellent above 90, Good above 80, Satisfactory above 60, Fair above 40, Poor above 20, and Fail at 20 or below.

Where each input comes from

Parameter (weight)SourceHow good it is
Cracking (0.12)Probe scores turned into % of road area, calibrated on Attain's labelled boxesRanks stretches on Attain (Spearman 0.59); 90% range held on 27 of 28 test blocks
Ravelling (0.10)Same methodFailed its coverage test (22 of 28), so its range is doubled
Patching (0.08)Same methodRange only (26 of 28); no better than a constant
Potholes (0.16)Runs of pothole frames, each weighted by whether the scooter felt a jolt thereA jolt at 52% of pothole frames vs 24% elsewhere
Roughness, IRI (0.40)Vertical acceleration from the phoneUncalibrated: a 100 m range is typically about 2 to 13 m/km
Rut depth (0.14)Published Bengaluru measurements, 0 to 25 mmNot measured; the camera cannot see ruts

Monte Carlo means drawing each input many times from its range, computing the PCI each time, and reporting the middle value and the 5% and 95% points of all the results.

Key idea

The report gives an estimate with an honest range, not a single number it cannot back up. For the eight sample stretches the median PCI is 46.5 to 54.7 (Fair) and the 90% ranges are about 29 to 93, against strict ranges of 3 to 97 when unknown inputs are counted as 0 and 100. About half of the remaining range is roughness.

Design vs build

Planned: a PCI following ASTM D6433, with field-measured sample units and deduct values. Built: a PCI following IRC:82-2023, the Indian code, chosen because the comparison survey will follow it. It is an estimate until calibrated on a reference survey. Also, the photo labels still use the IRC:82-2015 type list, while the PCI uses the 2023 formula.

Watch out

Three equations printed in IRC:82-2023 cannot be used as printed (the major-district and rural pothole curve and two rural roughness curves); those terms are reported as "not computable". The code's urban worked examples also do not reproduce (off by up to 10 points), while the highway examples match within 0.53. The copy used was a third-party PDF; an official copy should be checked.

9How the system decides

GeoAI is not an agent that plans its own steps. It is a fixed pipeline: the order of questions is set in code, the model answers each one once, and fixed rules decide what happens with the answers. This makes every run finish and makes every decision traceable.

The model decidesFixed rules decide
Is it a road; Normal or Distress; which types; severity; description; yes/no per typeThe order of questions; the 0.80 threshold; when to send to review; the safety net; segment length; gate thresholds; what counts as the stretch PCI

Bounds that make it finish

  • Stage 1 may generate at most 12 tokens; each stage is asked once per image.
  • The worker retries a failed image at most 3 times, then marks it failed.
  • A video batch is resumable: completed segments are skipped on restart.
  • The condition report draws a fixed 1,500 samples per section and refuses paths longer than 25 km.

10The human in the loop

The system asks a person when it is unsure, not at random. Every answer below 0.80 confidence, and every confident "Normal" that the probe disagrees with, goes to the expert page. The expert sees the photo, the model's types, severity, description and scores, and can correct them.

Why the page is designed this way

  • Leases. Each browser tab gets 12 items for 10 minutes, renewed every minute. Two experts never review the same item, so no work is wasted and no answer overwrites another. In a test with 8 sessions racing for items, no item was handed out twice.
  • Outbox. A save is kept in the browser with an id until the server confirms it, so a bad connection cannot lose a review. If the same save arrives twice, the server returns the first result.
  • Real names. Reviewers must enter a real name and agree that it appears in the paper's acknowledgements. Placeholder names are rejected, because a review log is only evidence if we know who reviewed.
  • Guided tour. A 17-step tour on an example card that never saves anything, so new reviewers can learn without touching real data.
Watch out

Only one citizen upload has been reviewed so far, and no video segment. Without expert labels from Bengaluru, there is no local accuracy figure. This is the project's biggest open gap.

15Viva cheat-sheet

1. What does GeoAI do, in two sentences?

It takes road photos from citizens and video from a phone on a scooter, and an AI model says whether the road is damaged, what kind, and how sure it is. Uncertain results go to an expert, everything appears on a map with its source image, and for any stretch it writes a condition report with chainage and an IRC:82-2023 PCI estimate.

2. Why a vision-language model and not YOLO?

A detector needs thousands of boxed examples per class and can only name classes it was trained on. Our model names 18 IRC damage types from a prompt and gives a description. The cost is that we get image-level labels, not boxes, and each image takes seconds. We say this openly and compare with detectors by what each delivers, not by their published box scores, because the tasks differ.

3. You fine-tuned a model. Why is it not used?

The QLoRA adapter raised GAPs accuracy from 76.53% to 88.39% and RDD first-label accuracy from 48.66% to 66.06%. On Attain, from a different country, it scored 0 on potholes, while the base model with improved prompts scored 0.265. A model for Bengaluru must work on unseen roads, so we kept the base model.

4. How do you know when the model is unsure?

We read the model's own token probabilities. Stage 1 uses the two first-token probabilities for Normal and Distress. Stage 2 uses the geometric mean of the probabilities of the damage-list tokens only. Anything below 0.80 goes to an expert.

5. Why only the damage-list tokens?

When the description was included, fluent text looked confident even when the labels were wrong. On 407 labelled frames the whole-text score had AUROC 0.373, worse than a coin flip, and the list-only score had 0.744.

6. Is 0.80 the right threshold?

Not proven. At 0.80 only 4 of 60 outputs with a false-positive type were routed to review on Attain, so the threshold is loose for that error type. It must be recalibrated on Bengaluru expert labels, and every answer stores its scores so this can be done without re-running the model.

7. The model misses more than half of damaged GAPs photos. Is that acceptable?

On its own, no: recall is 42.9%, while precision is 96.5%. That is why a confident Normal is checked again by the per-type probe, the safety net, which caught 313 of 371 missed damaged frames on Attain. The weakness is real and we report it.

8. What is the per-type probe?

For each of 20 types we ask the model a yes/no question and read the probability of "yes". It doubled macro MCC on Attain, but its thresholds flagged ravelling on 200 of 204 Bengaluru photos. So it runs in shadow mode: scores are stored and used for review routing, but labels are unchanged.

9. How is a video frame placed on the map?

All sensors share the phone clock. We fit satellite time to real time, draw a smooth path through the positions using speed, cut every 5 m of distance, and give each segment its sharpest frame. Each segment stores its position uncertainty; the median was about 6 m.

10. What does the gate do, and does it work?

It is a cheap filter that decides which frames to send to the model, with a 10% random audit of the rest. On the first three drives it kept 92.1% of damaged segments but sent 90.9% of all segments, so it saves little. A stricter version reached only 87.8% recall, below our 90% target, so it was not adopted.

11. How is the PCI calculated?

We follow IRC:82-2023: a weighted sum of six scores, with roughness 0.40, potholes 0.16, rut depth 0.14, cracking 0.12, ravelling 0.10 and patching 0.08. Each 100 m section gets an estimate with a 90% range for every input, and a Monte Carlo draw gives the median and range of the PCI. The eight sample stretches came out Fair, 46.5 to 54.7.

12. Why is the PCI range so wide?

Roughness carries 40% of the weight, and a phone on a scooter cannot give an absolute roughness without a reference measurement. Rut depth cannot be seen by a camera. We show the range rather than invent a number. A reference survey would narrow the roughness range about threefold.

13. How do you know a detected pothole is real?

The scooter's accelerometer: a jolt above a speed-matched threshold appears at 52% of pothole-labelled segments and 24% of others. With a label precision of 0.43 measured on Attain, a labelled run is real with a probability of about 0.74 with a jolt and 0.09 without. This is an estimate, and riders steer around potholes, so a missing jolt does not rule one out.

14. What is your biggest weakness?

No local ground truth. Only one citizen upload and no video segment has an expert label, so every accuracy number comes from public datasets from other countries. The expert page is built for this, and it is the first next step.

15. Why IRC:82-2023 and not ASTM D6433?

The roads are Indian and the planned comparison survey will follow the Indian code. We started with IRC:82-2015 and moved to 2023 when we found it replaced the 1-3 rating with a 0-100 PCI. ASTM's full procedure also needs measured sample units we do not have.

16. How did you make the system fast enough?

Full-size photos needed 47.8 GB of GPU memory on a 24 GB card; Windows quietly moved memory to system RAM, taking 270.5 s per photo. Capping images at 1024 px brought it to 11.1 s with no measured accuracy loss. A video profile that skips record-only work cut normal frames from 13.7 s to 3.8 s with identical answers.

17. How do you avoid fooling yourselves?

Test splits and pass rules were written down before running tests, splits keep neighbouring frames together, and intervals use block bootstrap. Results are always compared with a constant baseline. Several ideas failed and are reported, including tiles, crops, Grad-CAM and a weight edit.

18. What about privacy?

Photos carry GPS and may show faces or number plates. Images stay on our storage services and no field imagery is released. Faces and plates must be redacted, and consent and licensing written down, before any public dataset.

19. What would you do with three more months?

Collect expert labels on Bengaluru photos and video, then measure local accuracy and calibrate the probe and the 0.80 threshold. Run the reference survey and calibrate the PCI with the comparison tool, which is already built and keeps a blind test half. Check chainage length against km-stones.

16Glossary

4-bit (NF4) weights
Storing the model's numbers in 4 bits instead of 16 so it fits one GPU; computation still runs in 16-bit.
Adapter (LoRA, QLoRA)
A small set of extra trainable weights added to a large model; QLoRA trains it on a 4-bit model.
Attain
A public Iranian dataset of road frames from phones on car windshields, with damage boxes and severity.
AUROC
The chance that a random correct case gets a higher score than a random wrong one; 0.5 is a coin flip.
Block bootstrap
Estimating an interval by resampling groups of neighbouring frames, so near-duplicates do not shrink it.
Chainage
Distance along a road from a reference point, written like Km 0+260.
Cloudflare tunnel
A service that gives the local server a temporary public web address.
Cloudinary
A cloud service that stores the citizen photos.
Confidence (field)
The geometric mean of the token probabilities of the damage list in the model's answer.
Confusion matrix
A table of right and wrong answers per true class.
DINOv2
A general image-feature model from Meta, used by the video gate.
Expert review
The state of a result waiting for a human to check it.
F1
The harmonic mean of precision and recall.
GAPs
A public German road-photo dataset labelled Normal or Distress.
Gate
The cheap filter that decides which video frames the model sees.
GIS
Geographic information system: software that shows data on a map.
GNSS
Satellite positioning in general (GPS and similar systems).
Grad-CAM
A method that shows which image regions most influenced a model's answer.
IMU
Inertial measurement unit: the phone's accelerometer and gyroscope.
IRC:82
The Indian Roads Congress code for maintaining bituminous roads; the 2015 edition gives our damage types, the 2023 edition our PCI.
IRI
International Roughness Index, in metres of vertical movement per kilometre; higher means rougher.
Lease
A short-term claim on a review item by one browser tab, so two experts never review the same item.
MCC
Matthews correlation coefficient: agreement between predictions and truth, 0 for a constant guess.
Monte Carlo
Estimating a result by drawing random inputs many times and looking at the spread of outputs.
Outbox
A queue in the browser that holds an expert's save until the server confirms it.
PCI
Pavement Condition Index, 0 (failed) to 100 (excellent).
Precision
Of the cases called positive, the share that are positive.
Probe (per-type)
A yes/no question per damage type, scored from the model's probability of "yes".
Prompt
The instructions and question given to the model with the image.
Qwen2.5-VL-7B
The open vision-language model GeoAI runs, with 7 billion parameters.
RDD
Road Damage Detection: a public dataset with four crack and pothole codes.
Recall
Of the truly positive cases, the share the model found.
Segment
A 5 m piece of a drive with one chosen frame and one answer.
Shadow mode
Running a component and storing its output without letting it change results.
Softmax
A function that turns raw scores into probabilities that add up to 1.
Stage 0 / 1 / 2
Is it a road; is it damaged; which types, how severe.
Supabase
A hosted PostgreSQL database for the citizen path.
Token
A piece of text, roughly a word or part of a word, that the model reads or writes.
VLM
Vision-language model: an AI that reads an image and answers in text.
WDDM paging
Windows moving GPU memory into system RAM when the card is full, which makes the model 10 to 30 times slower without an error.
Worker
The background process that takes pending photos and runs the model on them.
